# Law & governance atlas: sources and teaching boundaries

Reviewed: 6 November 2026. This is a dated educational reference, not a live legal database. Recheck treaty membership, amendments, commencement, implementing rules and filing procedures before relying on a provision. The WIPO Lex versions reviewed are the 2001 Copyright and Industrial Property Acts; their listing is not a certification that every later change is consolidated. The Industrial Property source includes staggered commencement information.

## How to study

1. Filter by category, then search or select a Bhutan status.
2. Open a card for its full name, inside/outside scope, exact treaty dates where applicable, official references and related labs.
3. Select two or three cards to compare. Use the asset/problem scenarios to practise choosing legal hooks.
4. Use Classroom memory aids for the Rule of 3 Contrasts; the simplified maps always need the detailed card and source.

## Clarifications made to the supplied classroom notes

| Topic | Teaching clarification |
|---|---|
| EU AI Act territorial reach | EU output use is one Article 2 trigger. EU market/service links and other specified roles can also matter. |
| EU fine headline | €35M / 7% is the Article 99(3) ceiling for prohibited-practice breaches; categories, undertakings and SME rules affect applicable ceilings. |
| Four risk levels | A teaching map, with separate general-purpose model duties; classification requires statutory tests and current application dates. |
| OECD status | Membership and adherence differ. Bhutan was absent from the checked adherent list; non-members can adhere. |
| UNESCO 2021 | A non-binding Recommendation, not a treaty Bhutan signed or ratified. |
| Treaty dates | WIPO: accession 16 December 1993, in force 16 March 1994. Paris and both Madrid instruments: 4 May / 4 August 2000. Berne: 25 August / 25 November 2004. |
| Madrid | Bhutan designation is available subject to eligibility and examination, not an automatic grant. Agreement and Protocol have separate cards. |
| PCT and Hague | Bhutan cannot be designated under either checked system. Use the national route for Bhutan protection and confirm current DoMCIIP procedure; this does not necessarily require a physical visit. |
| Paris priority | 12 months for patents, 6 for designs/marks after a qualifying first application. These are priority-claim periods, not deadlines for every first filing. |
| Copyright | Computer programs are listed in section 5(1)(a); only qualifying original UI expression is covered. Author is a physical person. Life-plus-50 has category exceptions. No blanket guarantee for AI output. |
| Patent exclusions | Mathematical and business-method exclusions are in section 4(3), not section 5(2). The 20-year patent term runs from filing and has conditions. |
| Confidential information | Contractual restrictions need valid formation and actual confidentiality/access safeguards; not every weight, prompt or datum automatically becomes a trade secret. |
| GDPR | EU-origin data contact alone does not establish Article 3 reach. Establishment, targeting/monitoring and transfer-related obligations need separate analysis. |
| Cyber incidents | Assess actual statutory, regulatory and contractual triggers; do not apply a universal 72-hour rule to Bhutan incidents. |
