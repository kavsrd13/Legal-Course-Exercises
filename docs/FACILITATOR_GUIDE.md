# Facilitator guide

Legal Technology, AI, Digital Assets & Governance

Audience: 3–4 legal professionals with limited technical knowledge. All scenarios, organisations, products, contracts and incident facts are fictional. The instructor should review current primary law before delivery. The programme is not legal advice and quiz results do not certify professional competence.

## Delivery and release

Share the course index or one direct lab link when needed. All published content, including quiz logic and facilitator themes, is public; link distribution is not access control. Students work individually, then compare decisions. For the negotiation and incident simulation, assign rotating roles. Offline/manual completion is available throughout; Lab 3 supplies a deliberately flawed simulated AI response.

Progress is local to each browser. Specify your actual collection channel before teaching: LMS upload, a controlled shared folder or another approved channel. Students export Markdown submissions and JSON backups. This site does not collect them or provide a teacher gradebook.

## Review approach

Use the rubric for a conversation rather than automated legal grading. For each criterion: 0 = no evidence; 1 = partial; 2 = clear and supported; 3 = strong reasoning, explicit uncertainty and an operational control. Explain any feedback using the submitted evidence. A learner may choose a different commercial position if the assumptions, legal limits and approval gate are defensible.

## Revalidation before teaching

- Confirm primary-law text, amendments, commencement and territorial/sector scope. Do not copy an AI Act application date from a slide without checking the current text.
- Verify local IP ownership and assignment rules, regulatory perimeter, privacy/cyber duties and e-signature requirements. Keep EU/UK comparisons distinct from Bhutan rules.
- Treat OECD, FATF and NIST as principles/standards or frameworks unless domestic law or contract gives a specific provision binding effect.
- Resolve whether any proposed live tool is approved. Use only the supplied fictional information.
- Check the index and each direct lab link in the deployed project path. Test a JSON backup, restore and submission export on the classroom devices.

## Lab-by-lab delivery notes

### Day 1 · Lab 1: IP Portfolio Audit

Method: Audit workshop. Time: 90 minutes.

You are Meridian Group’s legal adviser reviewing its fictional agri-tech venture, Himalayan Harvest Analytics (HHA). A distributor wants exclusivity for OrchardEye. The board needs an evidence-based IP register before it signs.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Classify the portfolio | 15 | A complete register that distinguishes evidence from assumption. |
| 2. Choose protection and priority | 20 | Five reasoned priorities, including a secrecy or ownership control. |
| 3. Resolve ownership and disclosure gaps | 20 | A practical evidence request and a disclosure-risk plan. |
| 4. Set the licence position | 20 | A six-point licence brief ready for the negotiation workshop. |
| 5. Commit the 90-day plan | 15 | An auditable register, ownership-gap list and prioritised action plan. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- Employee ownership requires the applicable law and facts; do not turn silence into a universal rule.
- Prioritise the interface assignment, manual contributors, brand clearance and exposed pricing method.
- Novelty, government-source reuse and database rights remain jurisdiction-specific verification questions.

**Review criteria**

- All ten assets classified with missing evidence identified
- Five priorities have commercial and legal reasons
- Licence scope and source-code continuity are controlled
- Plan has named owners, dates and budget

**Knowledge checks**

1. Which fact creates the clearest documentary ownership gap?
   Suggested answer: The paid freelancer has no signed assignment. Payment is evidence of the commercial relationship; it does not by itself establish every transferred right.
2. What makes the pricing method protection more credible?
   Suggested answer: Limit access, record permissions and use confidentiality terms. A secrecy strategy depends on actual operational controls.
3. What is the best exclusivity safeguard?
   Suggested answer: Measurable sales milestones and review rights. Performance conditions reduce the risk of a distributor blocking a market.

**Carry forward:** Keep the register and licence position for Lab 2 and the Day 5 roadmap.

### Day 1 · Lab 2: Technology Licence Negotiation

Method: Negotiation role-play. Time: 120 minutes.

Meridian’s venture DrukLex Solutions owns a contract-analysis product. Sengri Systems wants to distribute it to banks and public bodies. Work individually first, then negotiate with a partner. Both role briefs are available for this educational role-play.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Prepare your mandate | 15 | A clear mandate, including a reasoned walk-away position. |
| 2. Review all eleven terms | 25 | An eleven-row review with proposed wording or a documented fallback. |
| 3. Draft the priority protections | 30 | Six reviewable replacements and a continuity solution. |
| 4. Negotiate and record concessions | 30 | A term sheet that clearly separates agreement from open issues. |
| 5. Advise the sponsor | 20 | A negotiated term sheet and a concise approval brief. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- Background IP, new developments and licence rights are different concepts.
- Define net revenue, evidence rights and remedies together.
- An unapproved deal is not improved by labelling it agreed.

**Review criteria**

- All eleven clauses reviewed
- Six redrafts contain operational detail
- Four concessions and approval conditions recorded
- Recommendation explains remaining exposure

**Knowledge checks**

1. Why revise “worldwide, any purpose”?
   Suggested answer: It may exceed the intended product, field and territory. Scope should match the commercial rights intended; enforceability is a separate legal question.
2. Who should usually remain contractually accountable for sublicensees?
   Suggested answer: The main licensee under agreed flow-down terms. Written flow-down protections and continuing licensee responsibility make the chain manageable.
3. Which is a more workable software commitment?
   Suggested answer: Material conformity to agreed specifications with defined remedies. Testable performance and proportionate remedies make a promise useful.

**Carry forward:** Export the negotiated term sheet. Reuse its audit, liability and exit structure on Days 2 and 4.

### Day 2 · Lab 3: Verified AI Legal Review

Method: Guided practice. Time: 150 minutes.

Meridian is reviewing a fictional NorthStar SaaS agreement. Practise the Upload → Summarise → Flag → Draft pattern using an approved AI tool, or use the supplied simulated output if no tool is available. Every finding must be checked by you.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Choose a safe workflow and prompt | 20 | A reusable prompt with explicit source and review boundaries. |
| 2. Extract and compare | 35 | An obligation matrix and a verified four-clause deviation table. |
| 3. Audit the simulated claims | 35 | A corrected claim log that prevents unsupported legal advice. |
| 4. Triage review material and tool limits | 25 | A traceable document triage and a human-control boundary. |
| 5. Draft the verified review memo | 35 | A fact-checked review memo and a reusable verification process. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- AI-03 claims 1, 2 and 4 conflict with C7, C3 and C8. Claims 3 and 5 are supported. Claim 6 has no supplied authority.
- Source-restricted extraction and legal research are different tasks.
- Manual review is a full practice route when an approved AI tool is unavailable.

**Review criteria**

- Prompt includes four G-C-S-E elements
- Four obligations and four deviations have source references
- Six simulated claims independently checked
- Memo and VERIFY record show human accountability

**Knowledge checks**

1. What is C3’s actual renewal notice period?
   Suggested answer: 60 days. C3 states 60 days before renewal; the renewal date is not supplied.
2. How should the invented statute in AI-03 be handled?
   Suggested answer: Label unsupported and check official legislation. The source pack supplies no such authority. A research plan is safer than a fabricated conclusion.
3. Does a “confidential” label prove legal privilege?
   Suggested answer: No; a qualified reviewer must assess the communication and applicable law. Privilege depends on legal tests and facts, not just a label.

**Carry forward:** Use the verified issue table as your evidence discipline in Lab 4.

### Day 2 · Lab 4: AI Procurement & Legal Risk

Method: Risk-allocation clinic. Time: 110 minutes.

Meridian’s governance committee must compare TalentPulse HR AI and LexisDraft Legal AI. Follow the sample workbench’s five-question procurement approach. A single “risk tier” or contract cap cannot answer all the legal and operational questions.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Classify the tool and the use | 20 | A conditional classification memo with a separate local-law check. |
| 2. Check data and IP terms | 25 | Two drafted protections plus a testable due-diligence request. |
| 3. Check accuracy, assurance and liability | 25 | A risk register and a justified negotiation package. |
| 4. Set five redlines and the internal rule | 25 | Five usable clauses and an enforceable internal operating rule. |
| 5. Make the committee decision | 15 | A reasoned approval memo, risk grid, five redlines and one-line policy. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- TalentPulse’s recruitment decisions and EU deployment require a specific high-risk analysis; do not assume every AI tool has the same category.
- LexisDraft’s ordinary drafting purpose does not remove confidentiality, accuracy, professional or contractual risks.
- The GPAI layer is separate. Avoid universal caps, automatic uncapped fine reimbursement and unsupported competence certificates.

**Review criteria**

- Both tools assessed with scope and role
- Data and IP issues separately allocated
- Risk scoring and liability assumptions explained
- Five redlines and an operational human-review control
- Separate conditional deployment decisions

**Knowledge checks**

1. What should precede an EU AI Act classification?
   Suggested answer: Intended use, scope and actor-role assessment. The facts and applicable legal tests determine the analysis; a product label is insufficient.
2. Is a USD 5 million liability cap always required?
   Suggested answer: No; it must be justified and negotiated for the exposure. Fees, potential loss, remedies, insurance and applicable law inform the negotiation.
3. Does assigning output rights guarantee new copyright protection?
   Suggested answer: No; existence, ownership and third-party rights still need analysis. Contractual allocation and statutory protection are different questions.

**Carry forward:** Carry the risk register and five redlines into the privacy, cloud and capstone labs.

### Day 3 · Lab 5: Token Rights & Transaction Mapping

Method: Concept and transaction workshop. Time: 75 minutes.

Meridian advises Bhutan Orchard Co-operative on fictional Green Orchard Units (GOUs). A digital token is one part of the structure. Your task is to explain the actual rights and identify the parties responsible when the process fails.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Translate the technology | 10 | A five-term explanation a business colleague could use. |
| 2. Map the parties and rights | 20 | A transaction map showing separate legal and technical mechanisms. |
| 3. Classify provisionally | 15 | A reasoned provisional classification with five legal questions. |
| 4. Resolve the failure cards | 20 | Four safeguards covering data, custody, rights and code conflict. |
| 5. Brief the board | 10 | A clear rights explanation and an explicit launch gate. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- The unit is investment-like on the facts; final regulatory classification is outside this simulated decision.
- Custody and off-chain data are central dependencies.
- RMA materials are a source to recheck, not a blanket conclusion about every token.

**Review criteria**

- Five terms explained
- Five parties and four flows distinguished
- Provisional classification has explicit unknowns
- Four failure safeguards and launch gate recorded

**Knowledge checks**

1. What does one GOU represent on the facts?
   Suggested answer: A contractual revenue claim recorded digitally. TOK-01 grants a contractual revenue share and expressly excludes land/fruit ownership.
2. What is F1 primarily testing?
   Suggested answer: The risk of incorrect off-chain input. Code can execute correctly while relying on wrong source data.
3. What should resolve a code/terms conflict?
   Suggested answer: An agreed correction, precedence and dispute process reviewed under applicable law. Technical execution and legal obligations should be aligned before launch.

**Carry forward:** Use the rights map to challenge the token launch in Lab 6.

### Day 3 · Lab 6: Digital-Asset Due Diligence

Method: Due-diligence case study. Time: 120 minutes.

Meridian’s investment committee is considering Tshechu Stay Tokens. You must assess the offering, distribution chain and customer safeguards, then decide whether the proposal can advance. All parties and figures are fictional.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Define the transaction and perimeter | 20 | A transaction perimeter and targeted evidence request. |
| 2. Build the twelve-risk register | 35 | A twelve-risk register with evidence and residual-risk judgements. |
| 3. Design the AML and custody controls | 25 | A control plan aligned to the actual roles in the chain. |
| 4. Write conditions precedent | 25 | Five verifiable gates and corrected customer-facing principles. |
| 5. Make and defend the decision | 15 | An evidence-based launch decision and escalation plan. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- Hold/stop the current launch is strongly supported by the data-room gaps.
- Separate regulator engagement from any assumption of sandbox approval.
- The 6% pool share is not a guaranteed individual investment yield.

**Review criteria**

- Transaction roles and jurisdictions identified
- Twelve risks scored with evidence
- AML and custody ownership documented
- Five conditions are testable
- Decision identifies legal blockers independently of scores

**Knowledge checks**

1. Which claim is an immediate evidence concern?
   Suggested answer: Government-approved, guaranteed return. No approval or guarantee evidence is supplied; the claim could mislead investors.
2. Does “licence pending” establish permission to operate?
   Suggested answer: No; scope, status and applicable activity need verification. An application is not proof of a granted, applicable licence.
3. Can a low aggregate score override a legal blocker?
   Suggested answer: No. A scoring aid cannot authorise an activity or replace legal analysis.

**Carry forward:** Bring your launch-gate method to the Day 5 governance roadmap.

### Day 4 · Lab 7: Privacy, Data Mapping & DPIA

Method: DPIA workshop. Time: 120 minutes.

Meridian advises the fictional Mountain Education Foundation on its DrukID Scholar portal. The Foundation decides scholarship eligibility; suppliers operate the technology. You must assess necessity, responsibilities, transfers and a new facial-matching feature.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Map the data and actors | 25 | A data inventory and purpose-specific responsibility map. |
| 2. Test scope, basis and proportionality | 20 | A conditional legal analysis and a less intrusive design option. |
| 3. Assess harms and residual risk | 30 | A harm-focused DPIA and accountable approval gate. |
| 4. Review suppliers and international access | 20 | A supplier and transfer plan with explicit jurisdiction assumptions. |
| 5. Respond to the exposed-link incident | 25 | A completed DPIA-style assessment and conditional incident response. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- The facial feature needs a necessity test, error/appeal controls and a non-biometric alternative.
- Do not describe GDPR as Bhutanese law or SCCs as a blanket answer.
- Under the GDPR comparison, Article 33 and Article 34 have different thresholds; assess each.

**Review criteria**

- Eight data items mapped
- Purpose-specific roles and territorial assumptions stated
- Five harms have controls and residual assessments
- Transfer analysis distinguishes regimes
- Incident log and design gate are actionable

**Knowledge checks**

1. Who determines the programme’s eligibility purpose on the facts?
   Suggested answer: The Foundation. The Foundation sets the programme purpose and data requirements; supplier own-purpose processing needs separate analysis.
2. Does hosting a backup in Germany alone settle GDPR territorial scope?
   Suggested answer: No; the actual establishment, offering and monitoring facts need assessment. Location is relevant to the data map but is not a complete territorial-scope test.
3. What is wrong with indefinite retention here?
   Suggested answer: No purpose-based retention justification or review is supplied. Define and evidence an appropriate schedule, including legal holds and justified exceptions.

**Carry forward:** Use the data map and supplier requirements in Lab 8.

### Day 4 · Lab 8: Cloud Contract & Exit Review

Method: Clause clinic. Time: 110 minutes.

Meridian is considering the fictional JigmeCase Cloud service for legal files. Review the contract alongside the Day 4 data map. A promise is useful only if someone can demonstrate, monitor and enforce it.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Map exposures and service needs | 15 | A business-requirements brief tied to actual data access. |
| 2. Review all nine clauses | 30 | A complete, source-referenced cloud deviation table. |
| 3. Allocate shared responsibility | 20 | A responsibility schedule that closes gaps between organisations. |
| 4. Build the exit and deletion test | 25 | A usable exit clause and a verifiable portability test. |
| 5. Make the procurement recommendation | 20 | A contract review package with responsibilities and exit controls. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- 95% is a business concern on these facts, not a claim about a legally mandated uptime.
- Audit evidence and exceptional inspection rights can be negotiated together.
- Data ownership does not resolve permitted vendor use, privacy or exit rights.

**Review criteria**

- Downtime calculations and recovery needs explained
- Nine clauses reviewed with alternatives
- Six shared controls have accountable owners
- Exit plan has measurable acceptance tests
- Recommendation distinguishes blockers from trades

**Knowledge checks**

1. At 95% over 720 hours, what is the illustrative downtime allowance?
   Suggested answer: 36 hours. 720 × 5% = 36 hours before applying any contractual exclusions. At 99.9%, it is 0.72 hours.
2. What is a useful portability acceptance test?
   Suggested answer: A sample export is imported and reconciled with attachments and metadata. Portability must be tested against the receiving workflow.
3. Does vendor-owned infrastructure remove customer access-control duties?
   Suggested answer: No; responsibilities must be allocated and operated. Shared responsibility requires explicit ownership, evidence and coordination.

**Carry forward:** Use the incident and exit provisions in Lab 9 and the capstone.

### Day 5 · Lab 9: Cyber Incident Legal Tabletop

Method: Sequential incident simulation. Time: 120 minutes.

Meridian’s fictional DrukCare Claims unit suspects unauthorised access. Reveal the injects in sequence. Work as incident lead, legal adviser, security lead and communications owner; a solo learner can document each role’s decisions. This is a tabletop, with no live system actions.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. 08:10–08:25 • First report | 20 | A proportionate first-hour response with evidence preserved. |
| 2. 09:05–10:15 • Scope expands | 25 | A notification register with separate triggers and accountable reviews. |
| 3. 11:30 • Evidence at risk | 20 | A documented evidence hold and enforceable cooperation request. |
| 4. 13:00–15:20 • Public and insurer pressure | 25 | A factual holding statement and documented insurance response. |
| 5. Day 2 • Vendor account implicated | 30 | A chronological decision log, notification outline and three contract lessons. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- Notification analysis can need to start before all facts are certain.
- Containment, evidence and continuity must be coordinated.
- Do not assume one universal deadline, automatic privilege or established vendor liability.

**Review criteria**

- Inject decisions preserve chronology and evidence
- Five notification routes have separate triggers
- Public statement distinguishes facts and unknowns
- Insurance and recovery decisions have owners
- Three contract lessons arise from the incident

**Knowledge checks**

1. What should happen to the logs?
   Suggested answer: Preserve them with integrity and controlled access. Investigation, legal duties and the contract require evidence to remain available.
2. What is the appropriate public figure at 13:00?
   Suggested answer: Use verified facts and explain that scope is under investigation. The facts support a potential scope estimate, not a confirmed theft count.
3. Where should the insurer notice requirement come from?
   Suggested answer: Policy wording and applicable facts. Insurance conditions and regulatory notice tests are separate.

**Carry forward:** Carry the decision log and three contract lessons into the capstone.

### Day 5 · Lab 10: Legal Transformation Capstone

Method: Portfolio and board pitch. Time: 150 minutes.

Meridian’s Thunder Dragon Legal Office needs a controlled digital transformation. Use your previous lab outputs as evidence. Select a realistic pilot, evaluate vendors and pitch a 90-day plan that a sponsor could approve.

**Teaching flow**

| Step | Minutes | Learner output |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Select the pilot and success measures | 25 | A bounded pilot with five measurable proposed outcomes. |
| 2. Evaluate vendors and gates | 30 | A transparent vendor comparison with a separate gate decision. |
| 3. Integrate the week’s controls | 30 | A governance plan anchored to prior decisions and explicit gaps. |
| 4. Build the 90-day roadmap | 35 | An owned, budgeted roadmap with measurable gates. |
| 5. Prepare the board pitch and portfolio | 30 | A one-page roadmap, vendor decision, five-minute pitch and complete portfolio. |

**Suggested reasoning / discussion themes**

- Vendor B is a plausible provisional fit, conditional on evidence; it is not an automatic approval.
- Start with process and ownership; expand AI use only after a controlled evaluation.
- A good roadmap records dependencies, residual risks, training, measurement and an exit route.

**Review criteria**

- Pilot scope and five targets are measurable
- Weights total 100, assumptions are recorded and gates are separate
- Five domains draw on prior labs or state missing evidence
- Roadmap has owners, budgets and acceptance tests
- Pitch requests a concrete, conditional decision

**Knowledge checks**

1. What is a suitable first pilot on these facts?
   Suggested answer: Structured intake and approved templates with ownership tracking. A bounded, repeatable process addresses known baseline gaps and can be tested.
2. Can weighted scores override an unacceptable data-training clause?
   Suggested answer: No; mandatory approval gates are separate. Scoring compares options only after legal and operational gate questions are addressed.
3. Which metric best demonstrates a business outcome?
   Suggested answer: Missed renewals with a defined baseline and review period. Outcome measures connect a change to a practical legal-operation problem.

**Carry forward:** Export your complete portfolio and deliver a five-minute board pitch.

## Instructor prompts for deeper discussion

- What exact fact or clause supports your conclusion?
- Which part is an assumption or a legal research question?
- What would make you change your decision?
- Who owns the control, and what evidence proves it is working?
- Is this a legal blocker, a commercial trade or a governance preference?
- What must happen before approval, and who has authority to approve?

## Boundaries

Facilitator answers are indicative, not definitive legal conclusions. The interface validates field population and review markers; it does not assess drafting quality or determine compliance. Do not interpret a risk-product score as a probability of legal liability. All model-answer themes are visible to students in the published client code.

## Participant orientation before every lab

Allow 5–7 minutes from the daily instruction/synthesis allocation for each pre-read before the task timings. Every lab has a dedicated background, role, central decision, known facts, open questions, visible objectives, deliverables, glossary and source-reading list. The full text is in PARTICIPANT_PRE_READS.md.

Ask one participant to restate the scenario, another to explain their role and another to name the deliverable. Resolve misunderstandings before asking them to confirm the readiness statements and begin. The confirmations are self-reports, not proof of comprehension. The pre-read remains available inside the lab; older saved answers are preserved.
